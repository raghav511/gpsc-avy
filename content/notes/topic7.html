How GPSC frames medicine:

  • Numbers: BP 140/90, fasting glucose 126, HbA1c 6.5%, FEV1/FVC 0.7, thrombolysis 4.5 h.

  • 'Investigation of choice' and 'drug of choice': CT for acute stroke/SAH, spirometry for COPD, PTU in first-trimester hyperthyroidism, salbutamol for acute asthma.

  • 'All EXCEPT' and 'which is FALSE': the 2025 Medical Officer paper opened with one on diabetes.

  • Homoeopathic therapeutics: one disease, one leading remedy, one keynote. The therapeutics table near the end is built for this.

Hypertension and ischaemic heart disease

Hypertension is diagnosed at ≥ 140/90 mmHg on repeated clinic readings (Indian and ISH/WHO guidelines); ACC/AHA 2017 uses ≥ 130/80. Troponin is the marker of choice for myocardial infarction.

Drug side-effects asked often: ACE inhibitors → dry cough (bradykinin), angio-oedema, hyperkalaemia; amlodipine → ankle oedema; thiazides → hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia; beta-blockers → avoid in asthma.

  • Biomarkers: troponin I/T most sensitive and specific (rises 3–4 h, stays 7–10 days); CK-MB rises 4–6 h, normal by 48–72 h (useful for re-infarction).

  • Infarct territory on ECG: II, III, aVF = inferior (right coronary); V1–V4 = anterior (LAD); I, aVL, V5–V6 = lateral (circumflex).

  • MONA-B immediate care: aspirin 300 mg chewed (all patients), clopidogrel/ticagrelor, nitrates (avoid in right ventricular infarct or hypotension), oxygen only if saturation < 90%, morphine for pain; plus statin and beta-blocker.

  • Complications: arrhythmia (VF — commonest cause of early death), heart failure, cardiogenic shock, papillary-muscle rupture (mitral regurgitation), septal rupture, free-wall rupture (days 3–7), Dressler syndrome (weeks later, pericarditis).

  • Secondary prevention: aspirin, statin (high-intensity: atorvastatin 40–80 mg; any time of day — MO 2024), beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor, stop smoking.

  • Heart failure basics: breathlessness, orthopnoea, PND, raised JVP, basal crepitations, pedal oedema; BNP/NT-proBNP raised; echo for ejection fraction; furosemide for congestion and acute pulmonary oedema (MO 2024).

Memory hook: ACE inhibitors give an Annoying cough (bradykinin). After an MI, troponin is the tortoise: it rises in a few hours and stays for about a week or two; CK-MB is the hare: gone in 2 to 3 days, so it is the marker for a fresh re-infarction.

Bronchial asthma and COPD

The one-line difference: asthma is variable and reversible airflow obstruction (inflammation, often allergic, young); COPD is persistent, not fully reversible obstruction (smoking or biomass smoke, older).

Definitions

  • Chronic bronchitis: productive cough for ≥ 3 months a year for 2 consecutive years ('blue bloater').

  • Emphysema: permanent enlargement of airspaces distal to terminal bronchioles with wall destruction ('pink puffer'). Centriacinar = smokers (upper lobes); panacinar = alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (lower lobes, young).

  • Status asthmaticus / acute severe asthma: PEF 33–50% predicted, can't complete sentences, pulse ≥ 110, RR ≥ 25. Life-threatening: PEF < 33%, SpO2 < 92%, silent chest, cyanosis, normal or rising PaCO2.

Complications of COPD: cor pulmonale (right heart failure, raised JVP, oedema), secondary polycythaemia, type 2 respiratory failure (raised CO2: bounding pulse, flapping tremor, headache), pneumothorax from bullae.

Related respiratory facts often clubbed in: pulmonary TB — cough > 2 weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss, haemoptysis; NAAT (CBNAAT/TrueNat) is the initial test under NTEP. Community-acquired pneumonia — commonest organism Streptococcus pneumoniae; CURB-65 for severity. Bronchiectasis — copious foul sputum; HRCT is investigation of choice; 'signet ring' sign.

Memory hook: Asthma comes and goes Again (variable, reversible); COPD Clings (persistent). An FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.7 means obstruction; a rise after a bronchodilator means asthma.

Stroke and epilepsy

Non-contrast CT is the first investigation in every acute stroke: it separates haemorrhage from infarction before any clot-buster is given.

Subarachnoid haemorrhage: 'worst headache of my life' (thunderclap), neck stiffness, photophobia; commonest cause ruptured berry aneurysm (circle of Willis, anterior communicating artery). CT first (MO 2025); if negative, lumbar puncture after 12 h for xanthochromia. Nimodipine prevents vasospasm.

Bell's palsy (LMN facial palsy): whole half of face including forehead; steroids within 72 h. In UMN (stroke) the forehead is spared.

Status epilepticus: seizure > 5 minutes or repeated seizures without recovery. Treatment: airway, glucose, then IV lorazepam (or midazolam/diazepam), then phenytoin/fosphenytoin, valproate or levetiracetam; if refractory, anaesthesia.

In India the commonest cause of acquired seizures is neurocysticercosis (Taenia solium; ring-enhancing lesion on CT). Investigations: EEG, MRI brain. Pregnancy: valproate is the most teratogenic (neural-tube defects); give folic acid. Phenytoin side-effects: gum hypertrophy, hirsutism, ataxia, nystagmus.

Memory hook: plain CT first: it rules out bleeding before any clot-busting drug. "Worst headache of my life" = subarachnoid haemorrhage. In facial palsy, an upper motor neurone lesion spares the forehead; Bell's palsy (lower motor neurone) takes the whole half-face.

Peptic ulcer, GERD and irritable bowel syndrome

H. pylori and NSAIDs cause most peptic ulcers; transient LES relaxation causes GERD; IBS is a functional disorder diagnosed by the Rome IV criteria with no structural disease.

Peptic ulcer disease

FrequencyCommoner (about 4:1)Less common
PainEpigastric, relieved by food, hunger pain, wakes at nightWorse after food, weight loss
CauseH. pylori in ~90%; high acidH. pylori ~70%, NSAIDs

  • H. pylori tests: urea breath test and stool antigen (non-invasive; best for confirming eradication), rapid urease (CLO) test on biopsy, histology.

  • Eradication: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin (or metronidazole) for 14 days; bismuth quadruple therapy if resistance.

  • Complications: bleeding (commonest), perforation (anterior duodenal ulcer; air under diaphragm), gastric outlet obstruction (projectile non-bilious vomiting, succussion splash, hypokalaemic hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis), penetration (posterior ulcer into pancreas).

  • Zollinger–Ellison syndrome: gastrinoma, multiple/refractory ulcers, diarrhoea; raised fasting gastrin.

GERD

  • Mechanism: transient lower oesophageal sphincter relaxations (MO 2024); hiatus hernia, obesity, pregnancy, smoking, alcohol, fatty food contribute.

  • Symptoms: heartburn, acid regurgitation worse after meals and lying down; atypical: chronic cough, hoarseness, asthma, dental erosion.

  • Diagnosis: clinical trial of PPI; endoscopy for alarm features (dysphagia, weight loss, bleeding, anaemia, age > 60); 24-hour pH monitoring = gold standard.

  • Treatment: lifestyle (raise head end, avoid late meals, lose weight), PPIs (most effective), H2 blockers; surgery = Nissen fundoplication.

  • Complications: oesophagitis, stricture, Barrett's oesophagus (intestinal metaplasia) → adenocarcinoma.

  • Oesophagus contrasts: achalasia = bird's beak, dysphagia to solids and liquids from the start; carcinoma = rat tail (MO 2025), progressive dysphagia solids → liquids.

Irritable bowel syndrome

  • Rome IV: recurrent abdominal pain on average ≥ 1 day/week for the last 3 months, with ≥ 2 of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form; onset ≥ 6 months before diagnosis.

  • Types: IBS-C, IBS-D, IBS-M (mixed); the commonest pattern keyed by GPSC is constipation alternating with diarrhoea (MO 2024).

  • Features that are NOT IBS (red flags): age > 50 at onset, rectal bleeding, weight loss, nocturnal diarrhoea, anaemia, family history of colon cancer.

  • Management: reassurance, diet (low-FODMAP), fibre for constipation, antispasmodics (mebeverine, dicyclomine), loperamide for diarrhoea, low-dose tricyclics; stress is a major trigger.

Memory hook: Gastric ulcer pain Gets worse with food; Duodenal ulcer pain is Dulled by food and wakes the patient at night. Most ulcers trace back to H. pylori or NSAIDs.

Hepatitis, cirrhosis and cholecystitis

Viral hepatitis transmission and serology, the complications of cirrhosis, and Murphy's sign with ultrasound carry most marks here.

Acute hepatitis: prodrome (anorexia, nausea, distaste for smoking), then jaundice; ALT > AST, often > 1000 U/L. Alcoholic hepatitis: AST:ALT > 2, raised GGT. Fulminant hepatic failure: encephalopathy within 8 weeks, prolonged INR (best prognostic marker). Paracetamol overdose antidote: N-acetylcysteine.

Cirrhosis

  • Causes: alcohol, chronic HBV/HCV, NAFLD/MASLD (rising), autoimmune hepatitis, haemochromatosis, Wilson's disease (Kayser–Fleischer rings, low caeruloplasmin), primary biliary cholangitis (anti-mitochondrial antibody).

  • Signs: palmar erythema, spider naevi (SVC area), gynaecomastia, testicular atrophy, Dupuytren's contracture (alcohol), clubbing, leukonychia (low albumin; MO 2024), jaundice, ascites, caput medusae, splenomegaly.

  • Severity: Child–Pugh (bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy); MELD for transplant listing.

Gallbladder: cholelithiasis and cholecystitis

  • Gallstone risk: 'fat, female, fertile, forty, fair'; cholesterol stones commonest; pigment stones in haemolysis.

  • Biliary colic: right upper quadrant/epigastric pain after fatty meals, radiating to right scapula.

  • Acute cholecystitis: persistent pain, fever, Murphy's sign (inspiration arrested on palpating the right subcostal area); leucocytosis.

  • Investigation of choice: ultrasound (stones with acoustic shadow, wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid). HIDA scan if doubt.

  • Treatment: analgesia, antibiotics, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (early, within 72 h ideally).

  • Complications: empyema, gangrene, perforation, Mirizzi syndrome, gallstone ileus.

  • Charcot's triad (ascending cholangitis): fever with rigors, jaundice, RUQ pain; add hypotension and confusion = Reynolds' pentad.

  • Courvoisier's law: a palpable, non-tender gallbladder with jaundice is unlikely to be due to stones — think carcinoma head of pancreas.

  • Acute pancreatitis: epigastric pain radiating to the back, relieved by leaning forward; lipase (more specific) / amylase > 3× normal; commonest causes gallstones and alcohol; Cullen's (periumbilical) and Grey Turner's (flank) signs.

Memory hook: viral hepatitis lifts ALT above AST; alcohol flips it, AST:ALT above 2 ("Scotch makes AST soar"). Gallstones love the 5 Fs: fat, female, fertile, forty, fair.

Diabetes mellitus

Diagnosis rests on four cut-offs: fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, 2-h OGTT ≥ 200, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, or random ≥ 200 with symptoms. Type 1 is ketosis-prone; type 2 patients die mostly of cardiovascular disease.

Chronic complications: microvascular — retinopathy (commonest cause of blindness in working adults; screen yearly), nephropathy (earliest sign microalbuminuria 30–300 mg/day; Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules), neuropathy (glove-and-stocking, diabetic foot); macrovascular — coronary disease (commonest cause of death), stroke, peripheral vascular disease.

Treatment and targets

  • Targets: HbA1c < 7% for most adults; BP < 130/80 (MO 2024); LDL < 100 (< 70 with heart disease); statin for most diabetics over 40.

  • Metformin = first-line oral drug; does not cause hypoglycaemia; side-effects GI upset, B12 deficiency, lactic acidosis (avoid if eGFR < 30).

  • Sulphonylureas (glimepiride, gliclazide): hypoglycaemia, weight gain. DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin): weight-neutral. SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): heart-failure and kidney protection; genital infections, euglycaemic DKA. GLP-1 agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide): weight loss. Pioglitazone: fluid retention, avoid in heart failure.

  • Insulins: rapid (lispro, aspart), short (regular), intermediate (NPH), long-acting (glargine, degludec). Insulin is the drug of choice in pregnancy.

  • Somogyi effect = morning hyperglycaemia after night hypoglycaemia; dawn phenomenon = early-morning rise from growth hormone and cortisol.

Memory hook: diabetes cut-offs read 126, 200, 6.5: fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or more, 2-hour (or random with symptoms) 200 mg/dL or more, HbA1c 6.5% or more.

Gout

  • Cause: hyperuricaemia (uric acid > 7 mg/dL men) from under-excretion (commonest) or over-production; triggers — alcohol (beer), purine-rich meat and seafood, fructose drinks, thiazides, low-dose aspirin, dehydration, surgery.

  • Acute attack: sudden, very painful, red hot swollen first metatarsophalangeal joint (podagra), often at night.

  • Diagnosis: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent monosodium urate crystals in joint fluid (gold standard). Pseudogout = rhomboid, positively birefringent calcium pyrophosphate crystals (knee).

  • Chronic: tophi (ear helix, elbows), 'punched-out' erosions with overhanging edges on X-ray, urate kidney stones.

  • Treatment: acute — NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids (do not start allopurinol during an attack); long-term — allopurinol (xanthine oxidase inhibitor; target uric acid < 6 mg/dL), febuxostat; lifestyle.

Rapid revision: 60 one-liners

Read this the night before and on the morning of the exam.

Numbers

  1. Hypertension ≥ 140/90; target in diabetes < 130/80.

  2. Hypertensive emergency ≥ 180/120 with organ damage; lower by ≤ 25% in the first hour.

  3. Salt < 5 g/day (WHO).

  4. Diabetes: FPG ≥ 126, 2-h OGTT ≥ 200, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, random ≥ 200 with symptoms.

  5. Prediabetes: FPG 100–125, 2-h 140–199, HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.

  6. HbA1c target < 7%; it reflects 2–3 months.

  7. Hypoglycaemia < 70 mg/dL.

  8. COPD: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70.

  9. Asthma reversibility: FEV1 ↑ ≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL.

  10. COPD oxygen target 88–92%; LTOT if PaO2 ≤ 55 mmHg, ≥ 15 h/day.

  11. Stroke thrombolysis within 4.5 h; status epilepticus > 5 min.

  12. GCS 3–15; ≤ 8 = intubate.

  13. Uric acid target < 6 mg/dL on allopurinol.

  14. SAM: MUAC < 11.5 cm.

  15. Microalbuminuria 30–300 mg/day.

Drugs of choice

  1. Acute asthma: salbutamol (inhaled SABA).

  2. Asthma controller: inhaled corticosteroid.

  3. Anaphylaxis: IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000).

  4. Type 2 diabetes first line: metformin; pregnancy: insulin.

  5. DKA: IV normal saline first, then insulin; watch potassium.

  6. Hyperthyroidism in first trimester: propylthiouracil.

  7. Hypothyroidism: levothyroxine on an empty stomach.

  8. Absence seizures: ethosuximide; status epilepticus: IV lorazepam.

  9. Hepatic encephalopathy: lactulose (+ rifaximin).

  10. Ascites: spironolactone.

  11. Variceal bleed: octreotide/terlipressin + band ligation; prevention: propranolol.

  12. Acute gout: NSAIDs/colchicine; long-term: allopurinol.

  13. H. pylori: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin.

  14. Scabies: permethrin 5%.

  15. Scrub typhus and leptospirosis: doxycycline.

  16. Paracetamol poisoning: N-acetylcysteine.

  17. Hypertension in pregnancy: labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa (no ACE inhibitors).

Investigations of choice

  1. Acute stroke / SAH: non-contrast CT.

  2. Gallstones: ultrasound.

  3. GERD gold standard: 24-h pH study.

  4. Bronchiectasis: HRCT.

  5. Gout: urate crystals in joint fluid.

  6. Thyroid screening: TSH; thyroid nodule: FNAC.

  7. MI: troponin.

  8. Pulmonary TB (NTEP): NAAT (CBNAAT/TrueNat).

  9. Dengue day 1–5: NS1.

  10. Heart failure marker: BNP.

Signs and associations

  1. Koplik spots = measles; strawberry tongue = scarlet fever; slapped cheek = parvovirus B19.

  2. Rash on day 1 chickenpox, day 4 measles; rash as fever falls = roseola.

  3. Auspitz sign, Koebner phenomenon, nail pitting = psoriasis.

  4. Murphy's sign = acute cholecystitis; Charcot's triad = cholangitis.

  5. Asterixis = hepatic encephalopathy (also CO2 retention, uraemia).

  6. Kussmaul breathing + fruity breath = DKA.

  7. Exophthalmos + pretibial myxoedema = Graves'.

  8. Podagra = gout; needle-shaped negatively birefringent crystals.

  9. Pellagra = dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia.

  10. Dry cough with ACE inhibitors; ankle oedema with amlodipine.

Traps

  1. Type 1, not type 2, diabetes is ketosis-prone.

  2. Duodenal ulcer pain is relieved by food; gastric ulcer pain worsens.

  3. Bell's palsy involves the forehead; stroke (UMN) spares it.

  4. Valproate is the most teratogenic antiepileptic; carbamazepine worsens myoclonic epilepsy.

  5. Avoid aspirin/NSAIDs in dengue.

  6. Do not start allopurinol during an acute gout attack.

  7. Rhus tox: better from continued motion; Bryonia: worse from any motion.

  8. Arsenicum asthma is after midnight; Natrum sulph asthma is 4–5 a.m. in damp weather.

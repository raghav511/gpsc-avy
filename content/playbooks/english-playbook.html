Your syllabus

What Sections I and II will look like

Most 2026 GPSC papers have comprehension only. Twelve 2026 papers had exactly your structure, with a separate 5-mark grammar block. They were for State Tax Officer, Mamlatdar, Scientific Officer, Welfare Officer, College Librarian, Under Secretary, Teacher and similar posts, held in January, February, March, May and September 2026. Those twelve papers are the model for your paper.

Section I · Comprehension · 10 marks

  • Two passages of about 120–190 words each (median 135; 2026 average 146).
  • Five questions per passage, one paragraph each, printed again with every question.
  • In the 60-question papers they sit at Q26–35, just before GK.
  • Topics are current and general: education, EVs, tourism, AI, climate, and also health. Homoeopathy and Biological Medicine passages appeared in Feb and Sep 2026.

Section II · Grammar · 5 marks

  • Five one-line items at Q46–50, right after GK.
  • Stems are short: "Choose the correct passive construction", "Identify the error", "Choose the correct inverted structure".
  • Each tests one rule. No passage, no long sentences.
  • Option E is always "None of the above", as in every GPSC CBRT.

What the papers show

Six rules for English in GPSC papers

1

Ideas replaced vocabulary

In 2021–24 a third of comprehension questions asked word meanings. In 2026 only 1 in 10 did. Main idea and tone now make up a third of the section.

2

Every passage follows one map

Q1 asks what the passage "primarily presents" or its central idea. Q2–4 ask details, an inference or a NOT question. Q5 asks the tone or the author's view.

3

The balanced option wins

Correct answers are qualified: "analytical and balanced", "cautiously optimistic", "benefits and challenges". Options with completely, entirely, solely, purely, only, always were almost always wrong in 2026.

4

Grammar tests structure, not definitions

A quarter of 2026 grammar items were inversion, subjunctive or comparison. Error-spotting is mostly subject–verb agreement.

5

Same item, many papers

GPSC reuses its English blocks across posts held the same weekend. The passive "They expect her to complete the task soon" and the "Hardly had he arrived when…" inversion each appeared more than once.

6

Answer keys lean to B and C

In 2026, 56% of comprehension keys were B and 31% C; grammar keys were mostly A–C. Use this only to guess when you have no idea; no marks are deducted.

The method

The 5-question passage map

10 marks · about 10 minutes

Memory hook: "First, Last, However". The main idea sits in the first sentence, the conclusion in the last, and the author's real view comes after However / Yet / Despite. In 2026 passages the key turn is often introduced by "However".

#1 · 32% of 2026 questions

Detail questions

"According to the passage…"

Sep 2026What aspect of education does the NEP prioritize in early childhood? Foundational literacy and numeracy

Jan 2026According to the passage, environmental laws fail mainly because enforcement is weakened by political and corporate interests

The right option is a paraphrase of one sentence. Wrong options borrow words from the passage but change the meaning, or add "all", "only", "complete".

#2 · 20%

Memory hook: "noun + with challenges". The answer names what the thing is and admits a limit. "A scientifically proven replacement for allopathy" (too strong) and "entirely unscientific and outdated" (too harsh) were both wrong for the homoeopathy passage.

#3 · 14%

Tone and the author's attitude

nearly always Q5

Of 37 tone questions in 2026, the correct option usually contained "analytical", "balanced" or "cautious(ly)". Pure praise or pure criticism was wrong.

#4 · 13%

The author's argument, solution or conclusion

"The author argues… / must…"

Jun 2026The author's final conclusion frames psychological well-being as: a fundamental human right requiring accessible, communal infrastructure

Jun 2026The main idea is that public health must pivot toward: structural interventions and primary care focused on prevention

Look at the last sentence. The answer usually combines two things ("decarbonization and resilience", "technological, legal and educational measures").

#5 · 10% (was 33%)

Vocabulary in context

"The phrase … means"
Phrase asked by GPSCMeaning in the passage

Memory hook: read one sentence either side. GPSC always explains the phrase in the next or previous sentence ("…that is, …", "such as …"). Never answer from the dictionary meaning alone: "silos" here are not grain stores.

#6 and #7 · 8% and 3%

Inference and NOT/EXCEPT

"suggests" · "NOT mentioned"

Inference

  • The answer is one small step beyond the text, never a leap.
  • Prefer "may improve" over "will eliminate".
  • GPSC Sep 2025: hypothesis of industrial civilization → "it is unlikely to be true", not "it is false".

NOT / EXCEPT

  • Tick off the three options you can find in the passage. The fourth is the answer.
  • The odd one is usually plausible but absent: "student resistance to technology" (NEP), "human preference for solitude" (Fermi paradox).

Sentence structure

inversion · subjunctive · comparison · cleft
RuleCorrect form (GPSC 2026 answers marked ★)

Memory hooks. "Hardly and Scarcely say When; No sooner says Than" (HS-W, N-T). For the subjunctive, the "W-words" (wish, would rather, were, as if, high time) all pull the verb one step into the past.

#4 · 14%

Tense and modals

"Choose the correct tense / modal"

Tense triggers

  • This is the first time I have heard … (present perfect) ★
  • By next month, she will have worked here for ten years ★
  • By the time X happened, Y had happened (past perfect)
  • Universal truth: water boils

Modals

  • The seminar is optional; you needn't attend ★
  • You should have apologized ★ (regret about the past)
  • She should have informed us earlier ★
  • mustn't = forbidden · needn't = not necessary

Memory hook: STOP sign vs open gate. Mustn't is a STOP sign (prohibition). Needn't is an open gate: you may pass or not.

Still possible

Older question types (2021–2025)

Papers before 2026 put vocabulary inside the comprehension block. These types still appear, mainly in 75-question papers.

Synonym / antonym of a phrase

  • "God-gifted quality" → God-given attribute
  • "convergence" ↔ divergence
  • "heed" ↔ not to pay attention
  • "Farthest in meaning" means choose the word that is not a synonym.

Statements true/false from the passage

  • "(i) … (ii) … Both true / (i) true, (ii) false…"
  • Check each statement separately against one sentence.

Sequencing

  • Arrange events in the order of the passage or of history.
  • Dandi March item: the letter to the viceroy came before the march.

Figures of speech

  • Paradox, euphemism, transferred epithet, rhetorical question, synecdoche
  • "If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?" → rhetorical question

A 13-day English plan

English needs about 25 minutes a day; most of your time belongs to the 25-mark core.

Days 1–4 · grammar rules

  • Read Section II above, one group a day.
  • English Drill 1 (grammar) on day 2, Drill 2 (vocabulary) on day 4.

Days 5–9 · comprehension speed

  • Two passages a day from mock sets 13–25 (real 2026 passages).
  • English Drill 4 (health passages) on day 7; Drill 3 on day 9.

Days 10–13 · full mocks

  • One full 75-minute mock a day. Aim for comprehension in 10 minutes and grammar in 3.
  • Re-read only the rules you got wrong.

All drills and mock sets are on the GPSC CBRT Mock Exam page. Mock Sets 1–12 now use real 2025–26 GPSC passages and 2026-style grammar; Sets 13–25 already did.

  • Stative verbs (have = possess, know, believe, like, love, want, seem, belong, understand) are not used in continuous forms: She has three brothers, not is having.
  • In time and condition clauses use the present for the future: I will call you when he comes (not will come).
  • Since + point of time (since Monday, since 2019); for + period (for three months).
  • It is time / It is high time + past tense: It is time we left.
  • Hardly / Scarcely … when, No sooner … than, with past perfect: No sooner had he arrived than it rained.

Subject–verb agreement

  • Each, every, either, neither, everyone, nobody take a singular verb: Each of the boys has a book.
  • Either … or / Neither … nor: the verb agrees with the nearer subject: Neither the teacher nor the students were late.
  • One of the + plural noun + singular verb: One of my friends is a doctor. But one of those who are (the relative clause refers to the plural).
  • The number of + singular verb; a number of + plural verb.
  • As well as, along with, together with, with do not change the verb: The doctor, along with the nurses, is here.
  • Collective nouns (team, committee, jury) are singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately.
  • Amounts, distances and time as one unit are singular: Five hundred rupees is enough.
  • Always plural: scissors, trousers, spectacles, cattle, police, people. Always singular: news, mathematics, physics, information, advice, furniture, luggage, scenery.

Articles

  • a before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound: a university, a one-rupee coin, a European; an hour, an honest man, an MBBS doctor.
  • the for something already known or unique: the sun, the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Indian Ocean, the Bible, the Taj Mahal; superlatives (the best); ordinals (the first); musical instruments (play the flute).
  • No article before names of meals, games, languages, subjects, most countries and single mountains/lakes (Mount Everest, Lake Chilka), and before school/hospital/church when used for their purpose (He is in hospital = a patient).

  • Time: at 5 o'clock / night; on Monday / 5 May; in May / 2026 / the morning. Between two, among more than two. Beside = next to; besides = in addition to.

Modals and conjunctions

  • Lest is followed by should: Work hard lest you should fail. (never lest … not)
  • Ought takes to: You ought to go. Need / dare as modals have no to in questions and negatives: You need not go.
  • Used to + verb (past habit); be used to + -ing (accustomed): I am used to working late.
  • Pairs: not only … but also, both … and, either … or, neither … nor, though … yet, as … as, so … that, such … that.

Direct and indirect speech

  • Reporting verb in the past: move tenses one step back (is → was, has → had, will → would, can → could). Universal truths and habits keep the present.
  • Words change: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, here → there, this → that, ago → before.
  • Questions: asked + if/whether or the wh-word, then statement order: He asked where I lived.
  • Commands: told/ordered/requested + to-infinitive: He told me to sit down. She requested me not to shout.
  • Exclamations: exclaimed with joy/sorrow that…

Question tags, gerunds and infinitives

  • Positive statement → negative tag; negative → positive: He is late, isn't he? You never lie, do you? Special: I am → aren't I; Let's → shall we; imperatives → will you; Everyone came, didn't they?
  • Followed by -ing: avoid, enjoy, finish, mind, deny, consider, suggest, admit, practise, risk, look forward to, object to, be used to.
  • Followed by to-verb: want, decide, hope, plan, agree, refuse, promise, afford, manage, offer.
  • Meaning changes: stop smoking (quit) vs stop to smoke (pause in order to); remember locking (memory) vs remember to lock (task).

Degrees of comparison and pronouns

  • No double comparatives: more better ✗. Latin comparatives take to: superior to, senior to, prefer to.
  • He is taller than I (am); between you and me; Who (subject) vs whom (object): the man whom we met.
  • Positive ↔ comparative ↔ superlative: Very few cities are as big as Mumbai = Mumbai is bigger than most other cities = Mumbai is one of the biggest cities.

How to answer in the exam

  1. Error spotting: check the verb first (agreement, tense), then pronouns, then prepositions and articles.
  2. Fill in the blank: read the whole sentence; look for signal words (since, by, if, lest, hardly).
  3. Vocabulary: eliminate the clear opposites first; pick the word that fits the tone of the sentence.

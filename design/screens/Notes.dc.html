Wounds, ulcers, shock and burns
Rule of nines, adult
Head and neck9%
Each upper limb9%
Front / back of trunk18% each
Each lower limb18%
Perineum1%
The patient's palm with fingers is about 1% of the body. Children have a larger head: use the Lund and Browder chart.
Pick the remedy by the tissue injured
Bruise, blunt injuryArnica
Nerve-rich partsHypericum
Puncture, bitesLedum
Torn open woundCalendula
Clean surgical cutStaphysagria
Bone, fractureSymphytum
Tendon, periosteumRuta
Deep soft tissueBellis perennis
Match the tissue first: a nail puncture is Ledum, a crushed fingertip is Hypericum, a fracture slow to unite is Symphytum.
Exam tip: Ledum wounds are cold to touch yet better from cold.
Telling the red eyes apart
Conjunctivitis
Gritty, discharge, normal pupil and vision · Euphrasia
Acute glaucoma
Severe pain, haloes, fixed mid-dilated pupil · refer urgently
Acute iritis
Photophobia, small irregular pupil · Merc corr, Rhus tox
Exam tip: a red eye with a fixed mid-dilated pupil is glaucoma until proved otherwise.
Rapid revision
1. Rule of nines: head 9, arm 9, trunk 18+18, leg 18, perineum 1.
2. Puncture wound, cold to touch: Ledum.
3. Fracture not uniting: Symphytum.
4. Clean surgical incision: Staphysagria.
5. Fixed mid-dilated pupil + pain: acute glaucoma.

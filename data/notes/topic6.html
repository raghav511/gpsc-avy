Apoptosis vs necrosis: apoptosis is programmed, single cells, no inflammation, cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation, apoptotic bodies, DNA ladder on electrophoresis, caspases. Intrinsic (mitochondrial, cytochrome c, BCL-2 family) and extrinsic (Fas–FasL, TNF) pathways. Example: Councilman bodies in viral hepatitis.

Free radicals and reperfusion: injury worsens when blood returns (reperfusion injury). Superoxide, H2O2, hydroxyl radical (most reactive); defences = SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, vitamins E, C, A.

Intracellular accumulations and pigments

  • Fatty change (steatosis): liver in alcohol, diabetes, obesity, kwashiorkor.

  • Lipofuscin = 'wear-and-tear' brown pigment of ageing (heart, liver).

  • Haemosiderin = iron; Prussian blue (Perls) stain; haemochromatosis.

  • Melanin; bilirubin; anthracotic carbon in lungs.

  • Hyaline: Russell bodies (plasma cells), Mallory–Denk bodies (alcoholic liver).

Memory hook: apoptosis is a quiet suicide (one cell, shrinks, no inflammation); necrosis is a messy murder (groups of cells, swelling, inflammation). The dying nucleus goes P-R-L: Pyknosis shrinks it, karyoRrhexis breaks it, karyoLysis dissolves it.

Acute inflammation is neutrophil-led and lasts hours to days; chronic inflammation is led by macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells, with tissue destruction and fibrosis going on together.

Cardinal signs: rubor, tumor, calor, dolor (Celsus); functio laesa added by Virchow.

Sequence of acute inflammation: transient vasoconstriction → vasodilatation → increased permeability (exudate) → stasis → leucocyte margination → rolling (selectins) → firm adhesion (integrins to ICAM-1/VCAM-1) → transmigration (PECAM-1/CD31, mainly in venules) → chemotaxis → phagocytosis → killing.

  • Chemotactic agents: C5a, LTB4, IL-8, bacterial products (fMLP).

  • Opsonins: IgG Fc and C3b (also collectins).

  • Most efficient microbicidal system: H2O2–MPO–halide (myeloperoxidase) system.

  • Chronic granulomatous disease = defect in NADPH oxidase (no respiratory burst); NBT test negative.

  • Chédiak–Higashi = defective phagolysosome fusion, giant granules.

  • LAD type 1 = integrin (CD18) defect, delayed umbilical cord separation.

Selectins make cells roll, integrins make them stop: MCQs swap these two.

Repair

  • Labile cells (skin, mucosa, bone marrow) always divide; stable cells (liver, kidney) divide when needed; permanent cells (neurons, cardiac muscle) cannot — they heal by scar.

  • Granulation tissue = new capillaries (angiogenesis, VEGF) + fibroblasts + inflammatory cells; it is the hallmark of healing.

  • Myofibroblasts cause wound contraction.

  • First intention (clean, sutured): epithelium covers in 24–48 h; collagen type III early, replaced by type I; strength ~70–80% of normal by 3 months.

  • Second intention: larger gap, more granulation tissue, contraction.

  • Delayed healing: infection (commonest), poor blood supply, diabetes, steroids, vitamin C deficiency (hydroxylation of proline/lysine), zinc deficiency, foreign body.

  • Keloid extends beyond the wound margin; hypertrophic scar stays within it.

Memory hook: the four Latin signs rubor, tumor, calor, dolor come from Celsus; Virchow added the fifth, functio laesa. White cells leave the vessel as "My Royal Army Takes Charge": Margination, Rolling (selectins), Adhesion (integrins), Transmigration (PECAM-1), Chemotaxis.

The three classics here are Virchow's triad, the commonest source of pulmonary embolism (deep veins of the legs) and the organs that get red versus pale infarcts.

Thrombosis

  • Virchow's triad: endothelial injury (most important, alone can cause thrombosis), abnormal flow (stasis or turbulence), hypercoagulability.

  • Lines of Zahn = alternating pale platelet–fibrin and dark red-cell layers; they prove a thrombus formed in flowing blood (ante-mortem). Post-mortem clots are rubbery, 'chicken-fat' over 'currant jelly', and not attached.

  • Commonest inherited hypercoagulable state: Factor V Leiden (resistance to activated protein C). Acquired: antiphospholipid syndrome, cancer (Trousseau migratory thrombophlebitis), pregnancy, OCPs, immobilisation, surgery.

  • Fate: propagation, embolisation, dissolution, organisation and recanalisation.

Stages: non-progressive (compensated) → progressive (lactic acidosis) → irreversible. Morphology: 'shock lung' (diffuse alveolar damage / ARDS), acute tubular necrosis, adrenal lipid depletion; Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome (adrenal haemorrhage) in meningococcal sepsis.

Memory hook: Virchow's triad is I-S-H: endothelial Injury, Stasis, Hypercoagulability. A thrombus forms in a living vessel, a clot can form anywhere, and an embolus is anything that travels and lodges.

The single feature that defines malignancy is invasion/metastasis; anaplasia is its microscopic hallmark. Carcinomas spread by lymphatics, sarcomas by blood.

Spread

  • Lymphatic: carcinomas; first node = sentinel node; Virchow's (Troisier's) node = left supraclavicular, gastric carcinoma.

  • Haematogenous: sarcomas, renal cell carcinoma (renal vein), hepatocellular carcinoma (hepatic vein), follicular thyroid carcinoma, choriocarcinoma. Liver and lungs are the commonest sites.

  • Transcoelomic: ovary; Krukenberg tumour = signet-ring metastasis to ovaries from stomach; Sister Mary Joseph nodule = umbilical metastasis.

  • Bone metastases: osteolytic mostly; osteoblastic in prostate (and breast sometimes).

Amyloidosis: extracellular deposition of misfolded fibrillar protein in a β-pleated sheet.

  • Stains: Congo red — pink-red, and apple-green birefringence under polarised light (the diagnostic test). Also crystal violet/methyl violet (metachromasia), thioflavin T.

  • Types: AL (light chains: multiple myeloma, primary); AA (serum amyloid A: chronic inflammation — TB, rheumatoid arthritis, bronchiectasis, osteomyelitis, leprosy); Aβ2-microglobulin (long-term haemodialysis); Aβ (Alzheimer's); ATTR (senile cardiac, familial); calcitonin (medullary thyroid carcinoma).

  • Biopsy sites: abdominal fat pad aspiration or rectal biopsy (least invasive); kidney is the commonest organ affected (nephrotic syndrome).

  • Spleen: 'sago spleen' (follicular deposits) and 'lardaceous spleen' (red pulp/sinusoidal).

Transplant rejection: hyperacute (minutes; preformed antibodies), acute (weeks–months; T cells), chronic (months–years; vascular fibrosis). Graft-versus-host disease follows bone-marrow transplant (skin, liver, gut).

Memory hook: hypersensitivity is ACID: Type I Anaphylactic (IgE), II Cytotoxic (antibody on cells), III Immune complex, IV Delayed (T cells). For antibodies: IgM is Made first, IgG alone Goes across the placenta, IgA guards the secretions, IgE causes allergy.

Tuberculosis and typhoid are asked most; leptospirosis and scrub typhus are the Goa monsoon specials.

Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)

  • Acid-fast, slow growing (generation time ~18 h), obligate aerobe; cord factor (virulence); grows on LJ in 6–8 weeks, faster in liquid MGIT culture.

  • Primary TB: Ghon focus (subpleural, lower part of upper lobe / upper part of lower lobe) + hilar node = Ghon complex; healed and calcified = Ranke complex.

  • Secondary (post-primary): apex of the lung (high oxygen) — Simon focus = apical seeding; Assmann focus = infraclavicular; cavitation; Rasmussen aneurysm causes haemoptysis.

  • Miliary TB: millet-seed lesions; spine = Pott's disease (thoracolumbar, cold abscess, gibbus); TB of the bone: best diagnosis = biopsy (MO 2025).

  • Diagnosis under NTEP: sputum smear for AFB (two samples) and NAAT (CBNAAT/TrueNat) as initial test; culture is the gold standard. Mantoux and IGRA show infection, not disease. ADA raised in TB pleural fluid.

  • MDR-TB = resistant to isoniazid + rifampicin; XDR adds fluoroquinolone + one Group A drug (bedaquiline/linezolid).

Leprosy (M. leprae): cannot be grown in culture (grown in mouse footpad, nine-banded armadillo); prefers cool parts and nerves (Schwann cells); slit-skin smear, ZN with 5% H2SO4; 'globi' in lepromatous; lepromin test negative in lepromatous, positive in tuberculoid.

Enteric fever (Salmonella Typhi, Paratyphi A and B)

  • Faeco-oral; incubation 10–14 days; step-ladder fever, relative bradycardia, rose spots, splenomegaly, leucopenia.

  • Pathology: Peyer's patches of terminal ileum; ulcers are longitudinal (oval, along the long axis of gut) — perforation in week 3. (TB ulcers are transverse/girdle.)

  • Investigation by week (BASU): week 1 Blood culture (also bone-marrow culture — most sensitive overall), week 2 Agglutination (Widal), week 3 Stool, week 4 Urine. Carrier site: gall bladder.

Leptospirosis (Leptospira interrogans) — common in Goa after monsoon paddy work.

  • Spread through rat urine in water/soil entering through abraded skin or mucosa.

  • Biphasic illness; conjunctival suffusion, calf tenderness; severe form = Weil's disease (jaundice, renal failure, bleeding); pulmonary haemorrhage.

  • Diagnosis: first week blood/CSF culture (EMJH) or PCR; after day 7 IgM ELISA; MAT (microscopic agglutination test) = reference; dark-ground microscopy. Treatment doxycycline/penicillin; doxycycline chemoprophylaxis for high-risk exposure.

Scrub typhus (Orientia tsutsugamushi) — transmitted by the larval trombiculid mite (chigger); painless eschar at the bite site; fever, lymphadenopathy, pneumonitis. Diagnosis IgM ELISA (Weil–Felix OX-K is old). Drug: doxycycline (azithromycin in pregnancy and children).

Memory hook: typhoid tests follow the weeks as BASU: Blood culture in week 1, Antibody (Widal) in week 2, Stool culture in week 3, Urine in week 4. TB is red rods on blue with Ziehl–Neelsen.

Dengue (flavivirus, 4 serotypes) — vector Aedes aegypti (day-biter, breeds in clean stored water).

  • Day 1–5: NS1 antigen or RT-PCR. After day 5: IgM ELISA. Secondary infection with another serotype risks severe dengue (antibody-dependent enhancement).

  • Labs: thrombocytopenia, leucopenia, rising haematocrit (≥ 20% = plasma leakage); positive tourniquet test (≥ 10–20 petechiae per square inch).

  • Warning signs: abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleed, lethargy, liver > 2 cm, rising haematocrit with falling platelets.

  • Chikungunya (alphavirus, also Aedes): severe, long-lasting arthralgia.

  • Japanese encephalitis: Culex; pigs = amplifier hosts, ardeid birds = reservoir; JE vaccine in UIP for endemic districts.

  • Kyasanur Forest Disease (flavivirus, tick Haemaphysalis spinigera; monkeys die first): reported in Goa's Sattari taluka and Karnataka's Western Ghats.

  • HIV: retrovirus (RNA → DNA via reverse transcriptase); gp120 binds CD4, co-receptors CCR5/CXCR4.
  • Window period ~3–12 weeks (shorter with 4th-generation antigen–antibody tests).
  • Indian testing strategy: three different rapid/ELISA tests for diagnosis (no Western blot needed).
  • AIDS = CD4 < 200/µL or an AIDS-defining illness.
  • Monitoring: CD4 count and viral load.
  • Infant diagnosis < 18 months = DNA PCR (maternal antibodies confuse ELISA).

Blood groups and transfusion

  • ABO antibodies are naturally occurring IgM; Rh antibodies are IgG (cross the placenta).

  • Universal donor = O negative (red cells); universal recipient = AB positive. Universal plasma donor = AB.

  • Most dangerous acute transfusion reaction = ABO incompatibility (acute intravascular haemolysis, type II). Commonest = febrile non-haemolytic reaction.

  • Storage: whole blood/PRBC 2–6 °C (CPDA-1, 35 days; SAGM, 42 days); platelets 20–24 °C with agitation, 5 days; FFP ≤ −30 °C (1 year). Cryoprecipitate = fibrinogen, factor VIII, vWF, XIII.

  • Rh-negative mother: anti-D at 28 weeks and within 72 hours of delivery of an Rh-positive baby; indirect Coombs test to monitor.

Renal function tests

  • Blood urea 15–40 mg/dL (BUN 7–20); serum creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL. Creatinine is better than urea; eGFR (from creatinine) staging CKD.

Glucose and diabetes

  • Diabetes: fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, 2-h OGTT ≥ 200, random ≥ 200 with symptoms, or HbA1c ≥ 6.5%. Prediabetes: FPG 100–125 (IFG); 2-h 140–199 (IGT); HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.

'Most common' and 'gold standard'

  1. Commonest type of necrosis: coagulative. Brain: liquefactive.

  2. Commonest embolism: pulmonary thromboembolism from deep leg veins.

  3. Commonest inherited thrombophilia: factor V Leiden.

  4. Commonest mutated gene in cancer: p53; commonest oncogene: RAS.

  5. Commonest site of metastasis: liver (then lungs); lymph nodes for carcinomas.

  6. Commonest anaemia: iron deficiency; best test serum ferritin.

  7. Commonest childhood malignancy: ALL.

  8. Commonest cause of infantile diarrhoea: rotavirus.

  9. Commonest cause of acquired epilepsy in India: neurocysticercosis.

  10. Commonest UTI organism: E. coli.

  11. Commonest site of amoebic liver abscess: right lobe.

  12. Commonest worm: Ascaris.

  13. Gold standard for malaria: peripheral smear. For TB: culture. For GFR: inulin clearance. For amyloid: Congo red with apple-green birefringence.

  14. Investigation of choice: gallstones = ultrasound; SAH = non-contrast CT; PE = CT pulmonary angiography; bronchiectasis = HRCT.

Pairs (stain, medium, test)

  1. ZN stain → AFB; Albert → diphtheria granules; India ink → Cryptococcus; Giemsa → malaria.

  2. Congo red → amyloid; Perls (Prussian blue) → iron; von Kossa → calcium; PAS → glycogen, fungi; Sudan/Oil Red O → fat.

  3. LJ → TB; TCBS → cholera; Löffler/tellurite → diphtheria; Sabouraud → fungi; EMJH → Leptospira; NNN → Leishmania.

  4. Autoclave 121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min; hot-air oven 160 °C, 2 h.

  5. Spore indicators: autoclave = Geobacillus stearothermophilus; hot-air oven = Bacillus atrophaeus.

  6. Endoscopes: 2% glutaraldehyde. Plastics: ethylene oxide. Disposable syringes: gamma rays. Sera: filtration.

  7. EDTA → CBC; citrate → PT/aPTT and ESR; fluoride → glucose.

  2. Langhans giant cells → TB; Reed–Sternberg → Hodgkin; Aschoff bodies → rheumatic fever.

  3. Negri bodies → rabies; owl's-eye inclusions → CMV; Councilman bodies → viral hepatitis (apoptosis); Mallory bodies → alcoholic hepatitis.

  4. Auer rods → AML; smudge cells → CLL; Philadelphia chromosome → CML.

Numbers

  1. Fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL; HbA1c ≥ 6.5% = diabetes; HbA1c shows 2–3 months.

  2. Haemoglobin: anaemia < 13 (men), < 12 (women), < 11 (pregnancy).

  3. TLC 4,000–11,000/µL; platelets 1.5–4.5 lakh/µL.

  4. Serum creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL; urea 15–40 mg/dL.

  5. Nephrotic proteinuria > 3.5 g/day; microalbuminuria 30–300 mg/day.

  6. Significant bacteriuria ≥ 10⁵ CFU/mL.

  7. HbA2 > 3.5% → β-thalassaemia trait.

  8. Mantoux ≥ 10 mm positive, read at 48–72 h.

  9. Transudate protein < 3 g/dL; exudate > 3 g/dL.

  10. Platelets stored at 20–24 °C (5 days); PRBC 2–6 °C.

  11. AIDS: CD4 < 200/µL.

  12. Rising haematocrit ≥ 20% in dengue = plasma leakage.

Classic traps

  1. Lymphoma, melanoma, seminoma, mesothelioma are malignant despite '-oma'.

  2. Typhoid ulcers are longitudinal; TB ulcers are transverse.

  3. ESR is LOW in polycythaemia and sickle cell disease.

  4. Hepatitis B is a DNA virus; A, C, D, E are RNA.

  5. HEV kills pregnant women; HCV goes chronic most often.

  6. IgM is the first antibody; IgG alone crosses the placenta; IgA is in secretions.

  7. Type 1 diabetes, not type 2, is ketosis-prone.

  8. Stage, not grade, predicts prognosis.

  9. Mantoux and lepromin test cell-mediated (type IV) immunity; lepromin gives prognosis, not diagnosis.

  10. Bleeding time is normal in haemophilia; aPTT is prolonged.

  11. PT/INR monitors warfarin; aPTT monitors heparin.

  12. Red infarcts in lung and liver (dual supply); white infarcts in heart, kidney, spleen.

  13. Prostate bone metastases are osteoblastic.

  14. Paget disease of the nipple is malignant.

  15. Little's area is anteroinferior on the nasal septum (GPSC's 2024 key is wrong).

  16. Mosquito is the definitive host of malaria; man is the intermediate host.

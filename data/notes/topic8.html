  • Postpartum haemorrhage (PPH). Blood loss of 500 mL or more after vaginal delivery (1,000 mL after caesarean).

  • Causes: the "4 Ts" — Tone, Trauma, Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (clotting).

Know each condition's definition, its classic triad or criteria, and one or two remedies with a clear picture.

Menstrual cycle in one line

  • Cycle 28 ± 7 days; flow 2–7 days; blood loss about 35 mL (over 80 mL = heavy).
  • Follicular phase (FSH, oestrogen) → ovulation on about day 14, 14 days before the next period, triggered by the LH surge → luteal phase (progesterone from the corpus luteum keeps the endometrium secretory).
  • Menarche about 12–13 years; menopause about 45–51 years (12 months without a period).

Abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)

AUB is any bleeding abnormal in frequency, regularity, duration or volume. FIGO classifies its causes as PALM-COEIN (diagram below): the PALM causes are structural and seen on imaging or histology; the COEIN causes are not.

Old termMeaning
Menorrhagia (heavy menstrual bleeding)Regular cycles, heavy or prolonged flow (over 80 mL or over 7 days)
MetrorrhagiaIrregular bleeding between periods
PolymenorrhoeaCycles shorter than 21 days
OligomenorrhoeaCycles longer than 35 days
AmenorrhoeaPrimary: no menarche by 15 years (with secondary sex characters) or by 13 (without them). Secondary: no period for 3 cycles or 6 months; commonest cause is pregnancy

The commonest causes of AUB at puberty and perimenopause are ovulatory (O); in a woman over 45, malignancy (M) must be excluded first.

Polycystic ovarian disease (PCOD / PCOS)

  • Rotterdam criteria (2003): any 2 of 3. (1) Oligo- or anovulation; (2) clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne, raised testosterone); (3) polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (12 or more follicles of 2–9 mm, or ovarian volume over 10 mL).

  • Features: irregular periods, obesity, acanthosis nigricans (insulin resistance), infertility. LH : FSH ratio often over 2 : 1.

  • Long-term risks: type 2 diabetes, endometrial hyperplasia and cancer, cardiovascular disease.

  • First-line management is lifestyle: a 5–10% weight loss often restores ovulation.

Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)

Ascending infection of the upper genital tract (endometritis, salpingitis, tubo-ovarian abscess), mostly Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Signs: lower abdominal pain, fever, purulent discharge, cervical motion tenderness, adnexal tenderness. Complications: infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain, Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis).

Very high unconjugated bilirubin can cross into the brain: kernicterus (bilirubin encephalopathy). Treated with phototherapy (blue light) and, if severe, exchange transfusion. Breast-milk jaundice appears after the first week and is harmless.

Infant feeding

  • Breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth; colostrum (first 3–4 days) is rich in protein, IgA and vitamin A.

  • Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then complementary feeding from 6 months, continuing breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond.

  • Breast milk has more lactose and less protein than cow's milk; whey : casein about 60 : 40; protective factors: secretory IgA, lactoferrin, lysozyme, bifidus factor.

  • Energy of breast milk about 67 kcal/100 mL; a baby needs about 150 mL/kg/day.

Behavioural problems

ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder). Inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity before the age of 12, in two or more settings (home and school), for at least 6 months; commoner in boys. Remedies for restless, destructive, hurried children: Tarentula hispanica (restless, destructive, music ameliorates), Stramonium (fears, violence, night terrors), Hyoscyamus (jealous, silly, exposing), Tuberculinum (wants change, destructive).

  • Enuresis. Involuntary wetting after the age of 5 years (developmental bladder control).
  • Primary (never dry) is commonest and often familial; secondary (dry for 6 months, then wetting) suggests stress, infection or diabetes.
  • Remedies: Equisetum (enuresis with dreams of urinating), Causticum (in the first sleep, also on coughing), Kreosotum (deep sleep, hard to wake), Sepia (in the first sleep, girls).

Other. Breath-holding spells (6 months to 5 years, harmless); night terrors; encopresis (soiling after age 4); pica (eating non-food items, look for iron deficiency).

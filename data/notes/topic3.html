Collecting plants

  • Collect from the plant's natural habitat, wild where possible, healthy and free from disease.

  • Collect in dry, clear weather, and use the plant fresh unless the monograph says dried.

  • Collect each part when it is most active: whole plants usually as flowering begins; seeds and fruits when fully ripe.

  • Identify the plant correctly by botanical name; the HPI monograph gives the part used and how to prepare it.

Vehicles

A vehicle is the inert medium that carries the drug. It must be pure, inert, and keep the drug stable.

Memory hook: 95, 99.4, 88: "Strong is 95, absolute 99.4, the dispenser is 88." Lactose dissolves in water, never in alcohol: so a 3C trituration is dissolved in water first, and alcohol is added after.

Preparing the neutral vehicles

A vehicle is neutral: medicinally inert, free from taste and smell, chemically stable, and able to carry the drug energy. Every vehicle is prepared and stored so that it stays pure.

VehicleHow it is prepared and kept
Distilled waterMade by distillation; used fresh to dissolve triturations (the 3C conversion) and to make dilute alcohols; stored in clean, glass-stoppered bottles
AlcoholStrong alcohol (95% v/v) is diluted with distilled water to the strength each step needs, for example dispensing alcohol (88%) for medicating globules
Sugar of milkPure, crystalline lactose, free from cane sugar and starch; ground to a fine powder and sieved before trituration; kept dry in closed containers
Globules, pilules, tabletsGlobules and pilules are made of cane sugar; tablets are pressed from lactose; all are medicated only after they are made, by soaking in the potency in 88% alcohol and drying

Memory hook: water dissolves, alcohol preserves, lactose grinds, sugar carries. Neutral means it adds nothing of its own.

Potentisation (dynamisation) is the step-by-step dilution of a drug with succussion (vigorous shaking) for liquids or trituration (grinding) for solids. Hahnemann describes it in Organon §§269 to 271; §270 gives the LM method.

Say the big numbers aloud, left to right: ten, hundred, fifty thousand. Then walk the bottom row: three hours of grinding before the drug ever meets alcohol.

The mother tincture (Ø) is the starting liquid preparation of a drug. 12C (24X) is roughly where Avogadro's number is passed, so no molecule of the original substance is likely to remain.

Trituration

  • Used for insoluble drugs (metals, most minerals, many animal products): the drug is ground with sugar of milk.

  • Each potency takes one hour: three rounds of 20 minutes, each of grinding and scraping.

  • Insoluble drugs are triturated to the 3C (6X); then the 3C is dissolved in water, alcohol is added, and the potency goes on as a liquid (conversion).

How LM potency is made (Organon §270)

  1. Triturate the drug to the 3C.

  2. Dissolve 1 grain of the 3C in 500 drops of a water and alcohol mixture.

  3. Take 1 drop of this, add 100 drops of alcohol and give 100 succussions: this medicates poppy-seed-sized globules, 500 globules per drop. That is LM 1 (0/1).

  4. Dissolve 1 such globule in a drop of water, add 100 drops of alcohol, succuss 100 times: LM 2. And so on.

The ratio is 1/100 × 1/500 = 1 : 50,000 per step.

Memory hook: "Ten, hundred, fifty thousand": X is 10, C is 100, LM is 50,000. Hering brought the X; Hahnemann made C and LM. One hour per trituration, three triturations, then convert.

Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia of India (HPI)

  • A pharmacopoeia is the official book of standards for drugs: identity, purity, strength and method of preparation.

  • The Government of India set up the Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia Committee in 1962, under Dr B. K. Sarkar.

  • HPI Volume I was published in 1971; further volumes followed. Before it, Indian manufacturers followed the HPUS (USA) and the German pharmacopoeia.

  • The HPI is listed in the Second Schedule of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, which makes its standards legally binding.

  • Most HPI mother tinctures are made by the new method, at a drug strength of about 1/10; Hahnemann's older method sorted drugs into classes by their juice content.

  • Drugs are tested at the Pharmacopoeia Commission for Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy (PCIM&H), Ghaziabad, which took over the Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia Laboratory.

Parts of a prescription

  1. Heading: date; the patient's name, age, sex and address; the prescriber's name, qualification and registration number.

  2. Superscription: the symbol Rx ("take thou").

  3. Inscription: the medicine, its potency and quantity, for example Bryonia alba 30, 2 dram globules.

  4. Subscription: directions to the pharmacist (how to prepare or medicate).

  5. Signatura (signature): directions to the patient, often marked Sig. or S.

  6. The prescriber's signature.

The two that get mixed up: subscription is for the pharmacist, signatura is for the patient.

Rules

  • Write clearly and in full; one remedy at a time, as the Organon teaches (§273).

  • Always state the potency, the scale and the dose form.

  • Name the medicine by its full Latin name, not a vague abbreviation.

  • Give the patient plain directions: when, how many, how long, and what to avoid (strong smells such as camphor or coffee, if the prescriber wishes).

Dosage forms

  • Internal: mother tincture, dilutions, triturations, medicated globules (pilules), tablets, cones.

  • External: ointments, lotions, glycerols, oils, liniments.

  • Medicating globules: pour enough of the potency, made in dispensing alcohol (88%), to soak all the globules in a clean vial; shake, then let them dry.

  • Storage: amber glass, well corked, cool and dark, away from strong odours and direct sunlight.

Posology

  • Posology is the science of dose: the potency, the quantity and the repetition.
  • The minimum dose (Organon §§275 to 279) is the smallest dose that cures gently.
  • Acute cases may need frequent repetition; chronic cases wait for the dose to act (see Topic 1, 1.3).

Memory hook: a prescription reads like a letter: date and address, "Dear pharmacist" (Rx), what to send (inscription), how to pack it (subscription), how to use it (signatura), signed by you.
